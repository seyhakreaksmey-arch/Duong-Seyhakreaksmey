The Hoover Dam is a concrete arch–gravity dam in the Black Canyon of the Colorado River, on the boundary between the U.S. states of Nevada and Arizona. Constructed between 1931 and 1936, during the Great Depression, it was dedicated on September 30, 1935, by President Franklin D. Roosevelt. Its construction was the result of a massive effort involving thousands of workers, and cost over 96 lives. Bills passed by Congress during its construction referred to it as Hoover Dam (after President Herbert Hoover), but the Roosevelt administration named it Boulder Dam. In 1947, Congress reinstated the name Hoover Dam. Since about 1900, the Black Canyon and nearby Boulder Canyon had been investigated for their potential to support a dam that would control floods, provide irrigation water, and produce hydroelectric power. In 1928, Congress authorized the project. The winning bid to build the dam was submitted by a consortium named Six Companies, Inc., which began construction in early 1931. Such a large concrete structure had never been built before, and some of the techniques used were unproven. The torrid summer weather and lack of facilities near the site also presented difficulties. Nevertheless, the dam was turned over to the federal government on March 1, 1936, more than two years ahead of schedule.

# Hoover Dam – Key Information

## 1. General Information

* **Location:** Black Canyon of the Colorado River, on the border between Nevada and Arizona, USA.
* **Type:** Concrete arch-gravity dam.
* **Construction period:** 1931–1936, during the Great Depression.
* **Dedication:** September 30, 1935, by President Franklin D. Roosevelt.
* **Original name:** Initially called Hoover Dam after President Herbert Hoover, but the Roosevelt administration renamed it Boulder Dam.
* **Official name:** Congress reinstated the name Hoover Dam in 1947.

## 2. Main Purposes

* **Flood control:** Helps control flooding along the Colorado River.
* **Irrigation:** Provides water for agricultural activities.
* **Hydroelectric power:** Generates electricity using water power.

## 3. Construction and Development

* **Project authorization:** Congress authorized the project in 1928.
* **Construction company:** Six Companies, Inc., won the construction contract and began work in early 1931.
* **Construction challenges:**

  * Extreme summer temperatures.
  * Lack of nearby facilities.
  * Unproven construction techniques for such a massive concrete structure.

## 4. Completion and Significance

* **Human cost:** More than 96 lives were lost during construction.
* **Completion:** The dam was handed over to the federal government on March 1, 1936, more than two years ahead of schedule.
* **Historical significance:** Hoover Dam was a major engineering achievement that advanced large-scale dam construction, regional water management, and hydroelectric power generation.
